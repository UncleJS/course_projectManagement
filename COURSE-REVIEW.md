## 2. Critical

**C1 — Closure report financial table does not sum** · `examples/16-project-closure-report.md:147-157`
The "Actual spend" column lists 231,800 + 46,500 + 42,000 + 19,400 + 33,200 + 3,200 + 0 (contingency) − 18,000 (parking saving) = **£358,100**, but the stated total is **£411,400** — a £53,300 gap. The table also double-handles CR-003 (the −£18,000 parking saving is shown as a line even though the GovTech "actual" may already reflect the reduced scope) and labels the £3,200 accessibility-testing line as **CR-002**, which the authoritative change log defines as the UNIFORM API change (+£7,200). *Manually confirmed.* This table needs to be rebuilt from a single consistent cost model — it is the worked example learners are most likely to copy.

## 4. Medium (81) — by theme

1. **Scenario-canon drift in the examples (~30).** Most notable recurring one: **Claire Worthington is called the "DPO"** in `examples/14, 16, 17, 19, 20`, but the stakeholder register makes her the **Section 151 / Finance officer** with the DPO a separate, unnamed STK-10. Others: Diane Hughes mistitled "Digital Communications Manager" (she is the Business Change Manager); a non-existent "Finance Director" role in the comms plan; project start 2 Feb vs 3 Feb; project reference NGL-2026-DIG-01 vs NDA-2026-003 vs a third 2025-dated format; OBJ-05 means different objectives in charter vs closure; "bulky waste" vs the delivered "missed/garden waste"; CR-003 logged as requested *by the supplier* (implausible for a scope reduction that releases its own money).
2. **Template ↔ worked-example structural divergence (~25).** A systemic pattern: many examples don't strictly instantiate their template — renamed columns (Lead→Owner, Chair→Facilitator, Date submitted→Date received), dropped sections (Implementation Instructions, Training Completed, Consolidated Demand-vs-Availability), and **status values used in examples that the template's controlled vocabulary doesn't define** (e.g. "Active" assumption status, "Closed — via CR-003" issue status, "Closed — Materialised" risk status, compound benefit statuses). **→ Scope decision Q4.**
3. **Genuine PM-content accuracy nits in modules (~12).** CCB wrongly equated with "Change Advisory Board" (an ITIL/CAB construct); RACI expanded as "Responsibility Assignment Matrix" (RACI is *a form of* RAM); "Accept = take no action" then told to prepare a contingency plan (conflates passive vs active acceptance); "contingency plan = fallback plan" (distinct concepts); a **Statistical Process Control chart shown under a "Pareto analysis" heading**; the risk-register template's P×I scale is incompatible with the scale taught in Module 11.
4. **Worked-example practice that mis-models the method for learners (~8).** Risk responses labelled "Accept" but with a *reduced* residual impact (acceptance doesn't lower impact); risks labelled "Mitigate" but closed as "Avoided" (should be "Expired"); a WCAG-compliance owner parked in the lowest stakeholder-grid quadrant; business-efficiency benefits owned by the ICT infrastructure manager; the CR-003 base-contract saving credited *into contingency*, inflating it above its original £38k.
5. **Glossary gaps & meta-file claims (~6).** Missing entries a learner needs: **Crashing** (referenced as a cross-ref but undefined), **Residual Risk**, **Secondary Risk**. INSTRUCTOR-GUIDE says "6–10 quiz questions per module" but modules 08–12 have 5.

## 6. Broken links (mechanical — 65)

Every module and several templates have **TOC links whose anchors don't resolve**. Cause: the v2.1.2 TOC expansion generated single-hyphen slugs for headings like `### Exercise 1.1 — Is it a project?`, but GitHub's slugger emits a **double hyphen** where the ` — ` em-dash sits (`#exercise-11--is-it-a-project`). All 65 were matched mechanically to exactly one correct target (zero ambiguous, zero unmatched) and are ready to auto-fix. This is the highest-volume, lowest-risk fix in the report.
