## Agenda Item 1 — Welcome and Introductions

James Hartley opened the meeting and welcomed all attendees. He noted that the Meridian Portal represents the council's largest single digital investment in five years and carries significant strategic importance for the Council Plan.

All attendees introduced themselves. Sarah Chen (PM) confirmed she had been in post since 19 January 2026 and has completed her initial project planning.

[↑ Back to top](#table-of-contents)

---

## Agenda Item 2 — Why This Project Matters

James Hartley presented the strategic context and business case headline:
- Current contact-centre volume: 14,200 transactional enquiries per year at £22/contact = £310,000 per year.
- Target: 55% digital deflection within 6 months of go-live; £170,000 annual saving by Year 2.
- Council Plan commitment: 60% digital transactions by 2028.

*Councillor Dean noted she had briefed the Cabinet Member for Finance and received positive support.*

*Ayo Mensah (Teams) asked directly about job security for the contact-centre team. James Hartley confirmed: "This project is about redeployment, not redundancy. We need skilled staff for complex casework that cannot be automated. Sandra will be communicating this to the team."*

[↑ Back to top](#table-of-contents)

---

## Agenda Item 3 — Project Scope and Objectives

Sarah Chen walked through the Project Charter scope sections:

**In scope (confirmed):**
- Planning application tracking
- Council tax payment and account management
- Bulky waste collection booking
- Parking permit renewal
- UNIFORM back-office integration

**Out of scope (confirmed):**
- Online planning application submission (Phase 2)
- Housing repairs / housing benefit (separate project)
- Mobile app
- UNIFORM system changes beyond integration points

*Mark Pearce asked for clarification on the UNIFORM integration: "We need to be very careful about what we change in UNIFORM — it underpins eight other council systems." Sarah confirmed the scope is read-only plus payment confirmation triggers, and that a detailed integration specification will be produced in the Discovery phase. Mark acknowledged this as acceptable.*

*Sandra Obi raised a concern about the council tax module — residents occasionally have complex accounts (multiple properties, split liability). She asked whether the portal would handle edge cases. Sarah Chen confirmed that the portal will handle standard accounts initially; complex cases will remain via the contact centre. This is reflected in the out-of-scope section.*

## Agenda Item 4 — Roles and Responsibilities

Sarah Chen presented the RACI matrix highlights. Key points noted:

- The Project Board (Sponsor, Senior User, Senior Supplier) will approve all changes above £20,000.
- GovTech will own delivery of the platform build but all delivery acceptance sits with council representatives.
- A Business Change Manager role needs to be filled — this is an open action (see Actions).

## Agenda Item 7 — Q&A

1. **Q (Ayo Mensah):** When will the contact-centre team be told more about the portal and what it will mean for their roles?  
   **A (Sandra Obi):** I will hold a team briefing in March, once we have more detail from the Discovery phase. The key message is redeployment opportunity.

2. **Q (Mark Pearce):** What access will GovTech have to our systems during discovery and build?  
   **A (Sarah Chen/Mark):** All access will be governed by the supplier contract and our standard third-party access policy. An NDA and data processing agreement will be in place before any system access is granted.

3. **Q (Claire Worthington):** How are we going to track benefit realisation — who owns that after the project closes?  
   **A (James Hartley):** Sandra Obi will own the benefits register as Head of Customer Services. The post-implementation review in April 2027 will be the first formal measurement point.
