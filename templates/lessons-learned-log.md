## How to Capture a Lesson

A good lesson learned has three parts:

1. **What happened**: A factual description of the event or situation (no blame; no opinion).
2. **Why it happened**: The root cause — not the symptom.
3. **What should be done differently**: A specific, actionable recommendation for future projects.

Lessons can be **positive** (things that worked well and should be repeated) or **negative** (things that went wrong and should be avoided or changed).

## Workshop Facilitation Guide

*Use at stage gates and project closure:*

### Preparation

- Circulate this log (with a blank or partially completed version) to participants 48 hours before.
- Invite examples in advance using the three-part structure: what happened / why / recommendation.

### Workshop Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Welcome; purpose and ground rules (no blame; focus on process not people) |
| 0:10 | Warm-up: one thing that worked well (round robin) |
| 0:25 | Discussion: what were the most significant challenges? What caused them? |
| 0:55 | Discussion: what should we recommend for future projects? |
| 1:15 | Prioritise top 5 recommendations |
| 1:25 | Agree action owners and submission to PMO |
| 1:30 | Close |

### Ground Rules for the Workshop

- Focus on **process**, not people — "the estimation process was flawed" not "Alex is a bad estimator."
- Specific examples only — generalisations are not actionable.
- Positive lessons are as valuable as negative ones.
- Every lesson needs a recommendation — "it was bad" is not a lesson.
